Feature 01 · The Principal

The engineer who taught
infrastructure to manage itself.

Since the late 1980s he has been making infrastructure run itself. The newest apprentice is a language model — trained one PowerShell script at a time.

SubjectMark Ringo
FiledAlberta Beach, AB
PracticeIndependent practice
StatusOperational, 35yr+

01 · Profile 12 min read

I have been building IT infrastructure since the late 1980s, and what is striking when I look at the arc isn't the variety of technologies — though there is plenty of that — but the consistency of the underlying instinct: find the waste, remove the manual step, make the system run itself.

It started with retail networks at Pier 1 Imports. Then Arthur Andersen, where I helped design server infrastructure and data center implementation for XM Satellite Radio, and architected directory services for the Chevron / Ultramar Diamond Shamrock energy merger. Hitachi Consulting hired me on its founding day. Seven years as Chief Technology Officer at Triencon Services — designing hybrid public-private clouds, deploying pervasive encryption, hardening Active Directory for electric grid control networks under NERC CIP. Citibank, as Senior Vice President of Global Infrastructure for North American Credit Cards Business Operations. Simmons Bank. Then Steeves & Associates, where the work converged on the strange and lovely intersection of cloud-native endpoint management, DSC v3, and the practice of training language models on the discipline itself. Since September 2026 I have been consulting independently again, under RingoSystems.

I live near Alberta Beach, where my home lab mirrors the enterprise patterns I deploy at scale — and where my cat, Chaos, continually stress-tests the exit door sensor automations. The work I am most engaged with right now sits at the intersection of agentic engineering and infrastructure: building tools that let tens of thousands of Intune-managed endpoints configure, secure, and remediate themselves, and training a custom LLM on the corpus of knowledge that makes that possible.

Lately that same instinct has opened a whole new line of work: Model Context Protocol servers built for a specific technology stack. Each one gives an AI client expert, hands-on command of one platform at a time, from Veeam Backup for Microsoft 365, to Homey Pro smart homes, to Reolink camera fleets. I ship much of this work free and open-source under the MIT license, published on Docker Hub and the public MCP Registry, because the fastest way to make infrastructure behave is to let everyone build on the same foundation.

008 Unreleased

Kamino

The cloners. Every silent install, proven on a disposable machine.

Takes any Windows installer, works out how to install it with zero user interaction, then proves it in a throwaway Windows 11 VM. The proof is the deliverable — no window appeared, no consent prompt fired, the process exited clean, the product was actually detected afterward. Ships the proven result as a WinGet manifest set and an Intune Win32 .intunewin with detection, install and uninstall rules. Running live on the bench, not yet publicly released.

Silent-install proof· libvirt / KVM· WinGet manifests· Intune Win32· PowerShell 7 / Pode

009 Unreleased

Kamino Imprint

Configure it once by hand. Get the configuration document back.

The configuration-capture product. After a proven package installs on a real bench machine, Imprint watches an operator configure the application and turns what changed into a DSC v3 or PSADT configuration document — instead of a screenshot-and-notes handoff. Checkpoints are stored separately and merged on read, so it knows which wizard page produced which registry write. Standalone, or step seven of the Kamino pipeline.

Toronto Utilities Atlas

Every large building in Toronto, coloured by what it burns.

An interactive 3D map of Toronto's large buildings, coloured and heat-mapped by the Ontario Energy and Water Reporting and Benchmarking dataset for 2018 to 2024. 012 Closed Pilot

Kyber on Understory

A knowledge system you will actually keep using.

A personal knowledge system built around one hard-won lesson: an earlier attempt, same person and same hardware and same purpose, was finished and then never adopted, because it was not simple enough to use. So the success metric here is adoption, not capability, and the day-one path needs no running infrastructure at all. The substrate is plain markdown, YAML and wikilinks in git — every tool is a view over it and is allowed to die. In closed pilot, under active development.

014 Recently Launched

RingoLLM

A narrow fine-tune that kept its tool calling.

Two published models, one domain. v5.1, a Muse Glimmer 30B fine-tune for DSC v3, PowerShell 7, Intune and agentic work, lifts DSC v3 accuracy from 56% to 89% while scoring 5/5 on tool selection and 15/15 on argument extraction — matching the untouched base model. Narrow domain fine-tunes in this space usually destroy tool calling. v4.2, the Qwen3.6-27B build, stays published for Legacy DSC 1.1, which v5.1 refuses by design. Both public on HuggingFace and Ollama.

Restraining Bolt

Limit the droid without disabling it.
